How an EIC show runs

Greenlight to delivery, without the handoffs.

Most shows lose weeks in the seams — between the entity and the payroll company, between prep and production, between wrap and the close. There are no seams here. The same team and the same records carry the show the whole way.

  1. 01

    Greenlight & Structure

    Week 0–2

    Before a single crew call, the show gets an entity, a budget it can actually shoot, and a plan for the money.

    • Budget build, schedule feasibility and pattern budget
    • Incentive strategy and jurisdiction modelling
    • Signatory posture, insurance and workers-comp placed
    • Chart of accounts, banking and payroll set up once
  2. 02

    Prep

    Prep through Day 1

    The crew, the vendors and the paperwork all land before the first shot — not during it.

    • Crew and vendor sourcing from relationships, not job boards
    • Start paperwork, I-9s and deal memos completed on a phone
    • Vendor certificates of insurance requested and tracked to expiry
    • Locations, permits, agreements and the safety plan
  3. 03

    Production

    Every shoot day

    The days run themselves because everyone has the same information at the same time.

    • Call sheets out the night before with live confirmations
    • A production line that answers at 2am so an AD does not have to
    • Daily production reports and hot costs before anyone sleeps
    • Incidents, injuries and changes logged the day they happen
  4. 04

    Wrap

    Last day + 30

    Wrap is a checklist, not an archaeology project, because the record was complete the whole time.

    • Asset returns, vendor close-out and final invoicing
    • Wrap book assembled from live records, not a shoebox
    • Union filings, residuals data and final cost report
    • Incentive audit package handed over clean
  5. 05

    Delivery & Close

    Through final acceptance

    Through picture lock to the network spec, on the date in the contract, with the list closed.

    • Post supervision and delivery item tracking
    • Music, clearance and legal deliverables
    • Technical QC against the network spec
    • Final close, distribution of records, lights off

About

Emmy Award-winning. Twenty-five years in.

Sean Alvarez is an Emmy Award-winning producer and production executive with more than 25 years of experience delivering high-caliber television and live event productions.

Now, as the founder of EIC Productions, Sean is launching a full-service production entity designed to support showrunners who want to stay focused on the creative, without the hassle of forming their own shingle.

EIC offers end-to-end production services at more than competitive rates, providing showrunners with the operational backbone they need while protecting their vision, voice, and ownership. With deep industry relationships, an impeccable reputation for delivery, and a calm, collaborative leadership style, Sean is creating a space where creativity remains in the hands of the creatives, and where a project’s ingenuity and execution are equally prioritized.

Before launching EIC, Sean most recently served as Senior Vice President of Production at Game Show Network and Game Show Enterprises Studios, where he oversaw all original series and specials. His prior leadership roles include SVP of Production at Fulwell 73, where he executive produced The Kardashians for Hulu and high-profile Disney+ specials like Encanto at the Hollywood Bowl and Elton John Live: Farewell from Dodger Stadium, as well as SVP of Production at Pilgrim Media Group, overseeing unscripted hits from My Big Fat Fabulous Life to Street Outlaws. Sean’s roots in the business began with blockbuster features like Con Air and Enemy of the State, and his career has been defined by a commitment to excellence, adaptability, and cultivating trusted teams. With EIC, he’s now channeling that legacy into a company that elevates the work of others.
